# Albums That Aren’t Just a List of Songs

These are not the greatest albums ever made or a list of everyone should love these or whatever. These are just albums that I think get across why I still listen to albums regularly instead of just playlists.

## Muse - The Origin of Symmetry

Muse is a weird band. I bought this on CD despite it being 2026. I have listened to The Origin of Symmetry front to back more times than I can count. I don’t know what it is about. Does anyone? And yet, it is best as a full experience. 

Listen if you like weird progressive-rock-classical-fusion and also falsetto that sounds like an electric guitar.

## Architects - Holy Hell

Holy Hell was written in the wake of the death of the Architects’ guitarist and main songwriter, Tom Searle. It is hard to describe how textural it is. It’s metalcore with electronic and orchestral bits, but also very clearly raw poetry.

Listen if you are interested in the sound of grief through critically acclaimed modern metalcore.

## Stupeflip - The Hypnoflip Invasion

I speak French. That has not helped me understand Stupeflip. They are abrasive, theatrical, experimental, and honestly half-fictional. They have lore. And yet, the flow is impeccable. There is also a My Little Pony fan animation video for Stupeflip vite !!! which inexplicably delights me.

Listen if you like French-DIY-rap-rock-theatrical-incomprehensible-audioshows.

## Laibach - Opus Dei

Laibach is a Slovenian (originally Yugoslav) art collective who use totalitarian, authoritarian, and martial aesthetics. Opus Dei features idiosyncratic covers alongside original music with both German and English lyrics. It is an uncomfortable album.

Listen if you are fascinated by fascist aesthetics, ambiguity, and industrial performance art.

## Slipknot - Vol. 3: The Subliminal Verses

Everyone has heard Duality and Before I Forget. There is a whole album and it oscillates between clean and harsh vocals and anger and melancholy and actual literature, in my incredibly biased opinion. 

Listen if you think you remember nu-metal on Kerrang! and wonder why I still care about it.

## If not Spotify

Spotify is fine, but if you care about media preservation or paying artists, you can buy albums from:

### New:

- The artist directly
- Bandcamp (especially on Bandcamp Friday)
- Local and independent shops
- Rough Trade
- Apparently HMV still exists

### Secondhand:
- Discogs
- Charity shops
