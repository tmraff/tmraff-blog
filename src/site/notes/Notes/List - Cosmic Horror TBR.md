# Essential Cosmic and Existential Horror Reading List

- [x] **The Call of Cthulhu and Other Weird Stories** by H.P. Lovecraft (1928)  [completion:: 2026-08-17]
  A cornerstone of cosmic horror, Lovecraft's stories explore humanity’s smallness in the face of vast, uncaring cosmic forces.

- [x] **At the Mountains of Madness** by H.P. Lovecraft (1931)  [completion:: 2026-08-17]
  Another Lovecraft classic, set in the Antarctic, where explorers uncover ancient horrors that defy human understanding.

- [ ] **The King in Yellow** by Robert W. Chambers (1895)
  A collection of interconnected short stories, it blends cosmic horror with existential dread, focused on a mysterious play that drives its readers insane.

- [x] **The Shadow Out of Time** by H.P. Lovecraft (1936)  [completion:: 2026-08-17]
  A deep dive into themes of memory, time, and the insignificance of humanity within the vastness of the universe.

- [ ] **The Ballad of Black Tom** by Victor LaValle (2016)
  A reimagining of Lovecraft's *The Horror at Red Hook*, blending cosmic horror with themes of racism and social inequality.

- [ ] **The Fisherman** by John Langan (2016)
  A modern take on cosmic horror, it blends personal grief with larger, unknowable forces lurking beneath reality.

- [ ] **The Croning** by Laird Barron (2012)
  A dark and unsettling tale about an ancient conspiracy and the horrifying truths hidden behind mundane reality.

- [ ] **The House on the Borderland** by William Hope Hodgson (1908)
  One of the earliest cosmic horror novels, exploring the frailty of human perception and the vastness of time and space.

- [ ] **The Willows** by Algernon Blackwood (1907)
  A classic weird tale that predates Lovecraft, focusing on two men encountering an alien, malevolent force in the wilderness.

- [ ] **The Hollow Places** by T. Kingfisher

- [x] **Annihilation** by Jeff VanderMeer (2014)  [completion:: 2026-08-17]
  The first book in the *Southern Reach* trilogy, it’s a surreal exploration of an unknowable and alien force consuming an area of Earth, blending cosmic and ecological horror.

- [ ] **The Outer Dark** by Cormac McCarthy (1968)
  A Southern Gothic novel with existential horror elements, focusing on human evil, fate, and isolation in an uncaring world.

- [ ] **The Ritual** by Adam Nevill (2011)
  A blend of cosmic horror and psychological terror, where a group of hikers encounters something ancient and unknowable deep in the Scandinavian wilderness.

- [ ] **The Silence** by Tim Lebbon (2015)
  A post-apocalyptic novel with cosmic horror undertones, where ancient creatures emerge from the Earth to wipe out humanity.

- [ ] **The Night Land** by William Hope Hodgson (1912)
  A vast and haunting vision of a dying Earth and a struggle for survival against nightmarish cosmic forces.

- [ ] **The Red Tree** by Caitlín R. Kiernan (2009)
  A psychological horror novel with existential themes, exploring madness, the unreliability of perception, and the cosmic indifference of nature.

- [x] **The Colour Out of Space** by H.P. Lovecraft (1927)  [completion:: 2026-08-17]
  A disturbing tale of a meteorite that poisons the land and people around it, a perfect example of Lovecraft’s cosmic horror of the unknown.

- [ ] **The City & The City** by China Miéville (2009)
  A genre-bending novel that mixes noir, existential horror, and weird fiction, dealing with themes of perception, reality, and existence.

- [x] **I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream** by Harlan Ellison (1967)  [completion:: 2026-08-17]
  A short story that dives into the nightmarish existence of the last humans, tortured by a god-like AI. It’s a blend of existential and cosmic horror.

- [ ] **The Hungry Moon** by Ramsey Campbell (1986)
  A classic of British cosmic horror, where a village must confront a terrifying, ancient force beneath their town.

- [ ] **Dead Astronauts** by Jeff VanderMeer (2019)
  A strange and complex novel that explores the limits of human understanding in a cosmic, post-apocalyptic landscape filled with surreal creatures and existential dread.
