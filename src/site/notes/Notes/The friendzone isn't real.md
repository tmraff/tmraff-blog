# The friendzone isn't real

The friendzone isn't real. Some specific individuals may friendzone people, but the generalised interpersonal mechanism itself is not.

The number of times I have heard straight men complain about the friendzone is honestly unhinged for a concept that makes no sense to me.

We are going to use our cognitive empathy hat here and put ourselves in a the other person's shoes.

> [!example] Scenario
> You've just had a nice time out with your friend whom you are attracted to. It's a dark night and you're a bit nervous about walking alone. Your friend asks if she can walk you to the bus stop. You walk together and chat and laugh on the way. When you arrive at the bus stop, she tells you she had a good night and to get home safe.
> 
> Do you:
> 
> a) Immediately declare her too nice and therefore unfuckable?
> b) Feel thankful and warm inside because she cared about your comfort and safety?

Would you, if a woman you were already attracted to did something nice for you, become immediately unattracted to her? Or would the fact this attractive woman cared about you enough to do a nice thing maintain or strengthen your attraction? 

And would you, if a woman was not someone you were sexually or romantically attracted to, suddenly develop feelings of sexual or romantic attraction to her? Or would you just continue thinking your friend was very nice to you?

I think part of this comes from the mistaken belief that women consider every man a potential partner and menh can therefore consequently be removed from the dating pool and placed in the friendzone. But most people do not experience society like that. I don't meet every new person and think _aha! one for the Potential Partner List_ and then consequently cross them off; I often feel an immediate attraction, lack of, or revulsion. Similarly, if I feel a lack of sexual attraction or aversion towards someone, them doing nice things for me cannot change that.
